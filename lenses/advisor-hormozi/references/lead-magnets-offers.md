# Lead Magnets, Offers & Value Equation — $100M Leads (Hormozi)

## Foundational Concepts

### Leads vs Engaged Leads

**Lead** = a person you can contact. Phone contacts, email lists, social followers, people on the street.

**Engaged Lead** = a person who SHOWS interest in the stuff you sell. They give contact info, follow you, reply to campaigns, book calls.

"Engaged leads are the true output of advertising." The entire book is about getting ENGAGED leads — not just any leads.

### Offers

**Source:** $100M Leads (Hormozi), Section II, Chapter "Engage Your Leads"

**Offer** = what you promise to give in exchange for something of value. A **core offer** promises your product/service in exchange for money. Advertising your core offer = going straight for the sale (the direct path to money).

"Try this way first." — Sometimes advertising your core offer is all you need.

## [100ML:LM] Lead Magnets

**Source:** $100M Leads (Hormozi), Section II, Chapter "Engage Your Leads"

**Core Principle:** A lead magnet is a **complete solution to a narrow problem**. It is typically a lower-cost or free offer to see who is interested. Once solved, it reveals ANOTHER problem solved by your core offer. "Think of it like salty pretzels at a bar. If somebody eats the pretzels, they'll get thirsty and order a drink."

**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: People want to know more before buying (especially expensive offers), CAC is too high with core offer alone, want to lower cost per engaged lead, want to build trust before selling
- AVOID WHEN: Your core offer alone gets enough engaged leads at acceptable cost (though lead magnets almost always improve results)
- COMBINES WITH: All [100ML:WO], [100ML:CF], [100ML:CO], [100ML:PA] — lead magnets are what you ADVERTISE through the Core Four

**Key Examples:**
- Hormozi's first lead magnet: a 13-minute case study video (replaced a webinar nobody watched). Overnight: "Strangers have booked my calendar solid for the next week"
- This book ($100M Leads) IS a lead magnet for Acquisition.com's core offer (investing in $1M+ businesses)
- Free 6-week gym challenge = Hormozi's most successful lead magnet ($1.5B+ industry impact)

### [100ML:LM.1] Seven Steps to Creating an Effective Lead Magnet

**Step 1: Figure out the problem and who to solve it for**
Pick a problem that is narrow AND meaningful. When solved, it should reveal a NEW problem your core offer can solve.

The **Problem-Solution Cycle**: Every problem has a solution. Every solution reveals more problems. This is the never-ending cycle of business.

Example: Help homeowners sell homes (broad). Narrow problems before selling: knowing home value, increasing value, pictures, cleaning, landscaping, staging, moving. Pick ONE narrow problem, solve it free → reveals: "they still have to sell their home" → your core offer.

**Step 2: Figure out how to solve it — Three Types of Lead Magnets**

### [100ML:LM.2] Type 1: Reveal Their Problem
Think "diagnosis." Works great when problems get WORSE the longer you wait.
- Website speed test → shows 30% below standard → clear line to lost revenue
- Posture analysis → shows current vs ideal → clear path to pain-free life
- Termite inspection → reveals damage risk → sell prevention or remediation

### [100ML:LM.3] Type 2: Samples and Trials
Full but brief access to your core offer. Limit uses, time, or both. Works great for recurring solutions to recurring problems.
- Faster server trial → lightning speed → keep paying to keep it
- Free chiropractic adjustment → relief → buy more for permanent benefits
- Costco food samples → consumables with limited uses and recurring need

### [100ML:LM.4] Type 3: One Step of a Multi-Step Process
When core offer has steps, give one valuable step free. Works for complex problems.
- This book: one step toward scaling past $1M/year
- Free first coat of garage sealant → need 3 coats for full protection
- Free finance courses → reveal the time/effort of DIY → sell financial services

**Step 3: Figure out how to deliver it — Four Delivery Methods**

### [100ML:LM.5] Delivery: Software
Give them a tool. Spreadsheets, calculators, small software, dashboards.
Ex: Business stats spreadsheet comparing to industry averages with ranking.

### [100ML:LM.6] Delivery: Information
Teach them something. Courses, lessons, expert interviews, presentations, live events, hacks/tips.
Ex: Mini course on how to write an ad.

### [100ML:LM.7] Delivery: Services
Do work for free. Audits, adjustments, first layer of service, video transformations.
Ex: Run ads for free for 30 days.

### [100ML:LM.8] Delivery: Physical Products
Give them something they can hold. Books, supplements, small tools, gear.
Ex: Gym Launch Secrets book for gym owners.

**3 types × 4 delivery methods = up to 12 lead magnets for a single narrow problem.** Rotate them to keep advertising fresh.

**Step 4: Test What To Name It**
"When you have written your headline, you have spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar." — Ogilvy

Test three things in order: headline, images, subheadline.

Hormozi's book title testing: Advertising vs Promotion (Advertising won) → Advertising vs Leads (Leads won) → Marketing vs Leads (Leads won). "How to" beat "Get" — two words made a measurable difference.

**Step 5: Make it easy to consume**
People prefer low effort. 2x-4x+ increases in take rates from easier consumption.
- Software: multiple formats (phone, computer)
- Information: video, text, audio, images — all formats
- Services: more times, more days, more channels
- Physical: simple ordering, fast delivery, easy to open

$100M Offers had near-perfect 1/4 split across ebook, physical, audio, video. "Making the book available in multiple formats is the easiest way I know to get 2-3-4x the amount of leads."

**Step 6: Make it darn good — Give Away The Secrets, Sell The Implementation**
"The marketplace judges everything you have to offer — free or not." Your lead magnet should provide MORE value than the cost of your core offer BEFORE they've bought it.

If you're scared of giving away secrets: "imagine the alternative: You give away sucky fluff. Then people think 'this person sucks!' and buy from someone else."

99% of people aren't gonna buy, but they will create or destroy your reputation based on the value of your free stuff.

**Step 7: Make it easy to tell you they want more — Call to Action (CTA)**
Good CTAs have: (1) what to do and (2) reasons to do it RIGHT NOW.

Three CTA enhancers:
- **Scarcity** — limited units/slots. Use REAL scarcity: "I can only handle five people per week, so if you want it solved soon, do xyz"
- **Urgency** — limited time. "July 4th promotion ends Monday at midnight"
- **Fraternity Party Planner** — make up ANY reason (Harvard study: giving ANY reason beats no reason, even bad ones). "Because it's my birthday and I want you to celebrate with me"

### Lead Magnet Economics

Even if a lead magnet costs money to deliver, it should LOWER your cost to get a new customer.

**Example without lead magnet:**
- $1000 per call from ads → close 1 in 3 → $3000 CAC

**Example with lead magnet ($25 to deliver):**
- $75 per call (because free offer gets more engagement) + $25 delivery = $100/call
- Close 1 in 10 → $1000 CAC
- **3× cheaper** than without lead magnet

"This happens all the time when you nail the lead magnet."

## [100ML:VE] Value Equation (for Leads)

**Source:** $100M Leads (Hormozi), Section II & III (referenced throughout)

**Core Principle:** Value has four elements. Maximize the top two, minimize the bottom two. The Value Equation applies to EVERYTHING — core offers, lead magnets, ads, outreach scripts.

```
            Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                    Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice
```

### [100ML:VE.1] Dream Outcome
What the person wants to happen, the way they want it. State the BEST possible results. Big bonus if results come from people like the prospect.

### [100ML:VE.2] Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
How likely they think it is FOR THEM. Include: results, reviews, awards, endorsements, certifications, 3rd party validation, guarantees.

### [100ML:VE.3] Time Delay
How long they believe it'll take AFTER they buy. Describe: speed to start, frequency of results, time to best results.

### [100ML:VE.4] Effort and Sacrifice
Bad stuff they have to endure + good stuff they have to give up. Show: good stuff they can keep, bad stuff they can avoid, and STILL get results.

**The Ideal (get as close as possible without lying):**
- You have exactly what they want
- They're guaranteed to get it
- Insanely fast
- Without lifting a finger or giving up anything they love

**Value Element Script Template:**
"I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (increase perceived likelihood — guarantee/proof)."

**What-Who-When Framework (for ads):**
- WHAT: 8 elements (4 value drivers + 4 opposites: nightmare, risk, time delay, effort)
- WHO: prospect + all status-relevant people (spouse, kids, rivals, boss, friends)
- WHEN: past experiences → present reality → future consequences

### [100ML:VE.5] Hidden Costs
If you can't give your stuff away for FREE, it means either:
1. People don't want the dream outcome
2. They don't believe you (perceived likelihood)
3. Hidden costs (time, effort, sacrifice) are too HIGH — your "free" is too expensive

To find hidden costs: ask "What would I have to do to make it worth it for you?"

**Metrics:**
- Engagement rate on lead magnet (% who take it)
- Consumption rate (% who actually use it)
- Conversion rate (% who buy core offer after lead magnet)
- CAC with vs without lead magnet

## [100ML:GA] Give-to-Ask Ratio

**Source:** $100M Leads (Hormozi), Section III, Chapter "Post Free Content Part II"

**Core Principle:** The ratio of value you give your audience vs the asks you make determines growth speed AND monetization potential. More giving = faster growth. More asking = more immediate money but slower (or negative) growth.

**Decision Algorithm:**
- APPLY WHEN: Building an audience, monetizing content, deciding how often to sell
- AVOID WHEN: N/A — always applies to content and audience management
- COMBINES WITH: [100ML:CF] (core content strategy), [100ML:LM] (lead magnets as "ask"), [100ML:AF] (affiliates need similar give:ask with their audiences)

**Key Data Points:**
- Television: 47 min giving / 13 min asking = 3.5:1 ratio (MINIMUM sustainable — mature platform focused on extraction)
- Facebook newsfeed: ~4:1 give:ask
- Growing platforms: dramatically over-give and under-ask

### [100ML:GA.1] Three Tiers of Give:Ask Strategy

**Tier 1 — Minimum Viable (3:1):** Mature platform model. Maximizes monetization but audience growth stalls or slows. Don't go below this.

**Tier 2 — Standard Growth (10:1+):** 10 give posts for every 1 ask. Audience grows while still making money.

**Tier 3 — Maximum Growth: "Give Until They Ask"**
Hormozi's preferred strategy. Never ask publicly — only respond when people come to you. "People are always waiting for you to ask for money. When you don't, they trust you more. They share your stuff more. You grow faster."

"Give in public, ask in private."

People who come to YOU are the BEST customers — they are natural "givers" who feel obligated to reciprocate. "If you advertise this way, your growth never slows."

"The moment you start asking for money is the moment you decide to slow down your growth. So the more patient you are, the more you will get when you finally make your ask."

### [100ML:GA.2] Two Ask Formats

**Integrated asks:** Woven into content (30-second spots in hour-long podcast). Keep above 3:1 ratio. Place CTAs after valuable moments or at end.

**Intermittent asks:** Separate "ask" pieces among pure "give" content. 10 gives → 1 ask. Better for short-form platforms.

**Warning:** A friend's podcast blew up, then he over-asked. It didn't just stop growing — it SHRANK. "Don't kill your golden goose."

**Metrics:**
- Audience growth rate (month over month)
- Engagement rate per post
- Give:ask ratio tracking
- Revenue per ask post vs audience growth rate
- If audience growth slows → you're asking too much

**Reversal:**
- "Give until they ask" requires patience and financial runway  <!-- privacy-ok: термин из книжного конспекта, не финансы владельца -->
- If you need immediate cash flow, you'll need to ask — just keep ratio high
- Never go below 3:1 or risk losing audience
