# Bullseye Framework, 50% Rule, Testing & Channel Selection

Source: *Traction: How Any Startup Can Achieve Explosive Customer Growth* by Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares (2015)

---

## [TRC:BF] The Bullseye Framework

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapters 3, 4, 5
**Core Principle:** A three-step framework for finding THE one traction channel that will unlock your next growth stage. Designed to systematically counteract founder biases and cast a wide net while zooming in quickly. "It is very likely that one channel is optimal. Most businesses actually get zero distribution channels to work. Poor distribution — not product — is the number one cause of failure." — Peter Thiel

### The Three Rings

#### Outer Ring: What's Possible (Brainstorm)

The first step is brainstorming EVERY traction channel. You must consider all 19.

**Rules:**
- Do NOT dismiss any channel as irrelevant — this is the critical mistake most founders make
- Come up with at least one idea per channel for how it could work for your business
- Imagine what success would look like in each channel
- Research what has worked in your industry and for similar companies
- Research how unsuccessful companies wasted their marketing dollars
- Write down your best channel strategy for each of the 19 channels

**The 19 Channels:**
1. Targeting Blogs [TRC:Ch1]
2. Publicity [TRC:Ch2]
3. Unconventional PR [TRC:Ch3]
4. Search Engine Marketing [TRC:Ch4]
5. Social and Display Ads [TRC:Ch5]
6. Offline Ads [TRC:Ch6]
7. Search Engine Optimization [TRC:Ch7]
8. Content Marketing [TRC:Ch8]
9. Email Marketing [TRC:Ch9]
10. Viral Marketing [TRC:Ch11]
11. Engineering as Marketing [TRC:Ch10]
12. Business Development [TRC:Ch12]
13. Sales [TRC:Ch13]
14. Affiliate Programs [TRC:Ch14]
15. Existing Platforms [TRC:Ch15]
16. Trade Shows [TRC:Ch16]
17. Offline Events [TRC:Ch17]
18. Speaking Engagements [TRC:Ch18]
19. Community Building [TRC:Ch19]

#### Middle Ring: What's Probable (Test)

Promote your most promising ideas from the outer ring. Usually 3 channels — stop at the natural drop-off in excitement.

**Rules:**
- Run cheap tests in parallel (NOT sequentially — waste of time)
- But not too many in parallel (lack of focus causes errors)
- Tests must answer three questions:
  1. How much does it cost to acquire each customer through this channel?
  2. How many customers are available through this channel?
  3. Are these the customers you want right now?
- Phase I: max $1,000 and 1 month per test
- Phase II/III: bigger tests because bigger numbers needed to move the needle
- DO NOT optimize at this stage — that's premature scaling
- Speed is the main consideration — get data fast
- Run 4 ads, not 40. You're determining IF it could work, not optimizing it.

**Noah Kagan's Spreadsheet Method (Mint):**
Track per channel: traffic estimate, CTR, conversion rate, expected customers, cost. Compare across channels to find the winner. Mint tested: targeting blogs, publicity, SEM → found targeting blogs was the core channel for Phase I.

#### Inner Ring: What's Working (Focus)

Focus ALL traction efforts on your single most promising channel — the core channel.

**Rules:**
- ONE channel only — stop all distracting marketing in other channels
- Wring every bit of traction out of it through continual experimentation
- Optimize strategies and tactics within the core channel (A/B testing, new approaches)
- Uncover new channel strategies within your core channel
- Use other channels ONLY to support your core channel (e.g., SEO as core → use publicity for links)
- When this channel gets saturated or too expensive, repeat the entire Bullseye process

**Common Mistake:** Keeping secondary channels alive because "they somewhat work." If SEM is significantly better than trade shows and publicity, focus 100% on SEM. The undiscovered strategies within SEM will have more impact than distributing effort across multiple channels.

### Why Bullseye Works

1. **Forces consideration of all 19 channels** — Most founders only consider 2-3 they know
2. **Systematically counteracts biases** — Unfamiliar channels are often the best (less competition)
3. **Zooms in quickly and cheaply** — Successive rounds of cheap parallel tests
4. **Finds underutilized channels** — "When we looked at companies really taking off, they were usually employing underutilized channels"
5. **Works with Lean** — "What Lean is to product development, Bullseye is to traction"

### Bullseye in Action: Mint.com

1. **Outer Ring:** Noah Kagan brainstormed all channels with quant spreadsheet
2. **Middle Ring:** Tested targeting blogs, publicity, SEM in parallel
3. **Inner Ring:** Targeting blogs emerged as core channel → 40,000 customers prelaunch
4. **Channel strategies within core:** VIP badges, direct blog sponsoring, content partnerships
5. **When blogs maxed out:** Repeated Bullseye → publicity became new core channel → 1M users in 6 months

### Bullseye in Action: DuckDuckGo

1. Started with SEO (bias from previous startup experience) → only 50 visitors/day
2. Mistake: no concrete traction goal + bias toward familiar channel
3. Shifted to content marketing, social/display ads, publicity, then business development
4. Each growth stage (100 → 1K → 10K → 100K → 1M → 10M searches/day) involved finding new core channel
5. "What works in one growth stage eventually stops working"

## [TRC:50] The 50% Rule

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapter 2
**Core Principle:** Spend 50% of your time on product development and 50% on traction. "Almost every failed startup has a product. What failed startups don't have is enough customers." — Marc Andreessen

### Why It Matters

**Four traps even good products fall into:**
1. Can't find viable business model (no market for monetization)
2. Market too small (too narrow a niche)
3. Reaching customers is cost prohibitive (e.g., cheap product requiring expensive sales force)
4. Hypercompetitive market (too many similar products)

Following the 50% rule from the beginning gives you the best chance of avoiding these traps. If you don't, you risk realizing you're in one too late.

### Benefits of Parallel Development

1. **Builds the RIGHT product** — Cold customers from traction experiments tell you what's actually broken. Early customers are too close and tell you what you want to hear. "You can think of your initial investment in traction as pouring water into a leaky bucket."
2. **Head start on traction** — When product is ready, you can grow rapidly. Marketo: SEO + blog before product → 14,000 interested buyers when product launched.
3. **Informs messaging and positioning** — Learn what resonates with real prospects.
4. **Identifies distribution roadblocks early** — Dropbox: tested SEM, found $230 CPA for $99 product → pivoted to viral marketing BEFORE launching.

### The Leaky Bucket Metaphor

- **Phase I (Making something people want):** Bucket has many leaks. Send small amount of water through to find and plug holes. Traction experiments find the REAL leaks.
- **Phase II (Marketing something people want):** Product-market fit achieved, customers stick. Scale up traction. Fine-tune positioning and messaging.
- **Phase III (Scaling your business):** Established business model. Focus on scaling channels and dominating market.

### Growth Happens in Spurts

"Startup growth happens in spurts. Initially, growth is usually slow. Then it spikes as a useful traction channel strategy is unlocked. Eventually it flattens out as this strategy gets saturated. Then you unlock another strategy and you get another spike."

### Moving the Needle

- Always focus traction efforts on activities that could meaningfully impact your traction goal
- "Does this channel have enough customers to be meaningful?" — simple spreadsheet calculation
- What moves the needle changes as you grow:
  - Phase I: first customers proving product can get traction
  - Phase II: enough customers for sustainability
  - Phase III: increasing earnings, scaling channels, creating sustainable business
- In later stages, smaller tactics stop moving the needle. 20 visitors from a tweet = noise when you have 10K/day.

### Key Quote
"This 50 percent rule is hard to follow because the pull to spend all of your attention on product is strong. After all, you probably got into your startup because you wanted to build a particular product or service. A lot of the traction activities are unknown and outside of both your comfort zone and this initial vision. That's why there is a natural tendency to avoid them. Don't."

## [TRC:TT] Traction Testing

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapter 4
**Core Principle:** Continuous testing is the key to getting traction. Middle ring tests find promising channels; inner ring tests optimize the chosen channel. Use cheap, fast tests designed to answer specific questions. Be scientific. Constantly look for new, untapped strategies to combat the Law of Shitty Click-Throughs.

### Middle Ring Testing

**Goal:** Find a promising channel to focus on.

**Design each test to answer:**
1. How much does it cost to acquire each customer through this channel strategy?
2. How many customers are available through this channel strategy?
3. Are the customers you are getting the ones you want right now?

**Phase I constraints:** <$1,000 and <1 month per test. Often cheaper and shorter.
**Phase II/III:** Tests may be bigger because you need bigger numbers to move the needle.

**Critical distinction:** Running 10 social ads and optimizing them = full-time job = OPTIMIZATION, not testing. Run 2 social ads with 2 landing pages = TESTING. Stay at the strategy level, not the tactics level.

### Inner Ring Testing

**Goal:** Two things: (1) optimize chosen channel strategy, (2) uncover better strategies within the channel.

**Optimization:**
- Each channel strategy has tweakable variables (blogs: which blogs, content type, CTA; SEM: keywords, ad copy, demographics, landing pages)
- Use A/B testing (split testing). Even one test per week = 2-3x improvement.
- Tools: Optimizely, Visual Website Optimizer, Unbounce

**New Strategy Discovery:**
- Once you focus on a channel, you become an expert in it
- Channel experts uncover new strategies that are often the best (because they're new)
- The Law of Shitty Click-Throughs: "Over time, all marketing strategies result in shitty click-through rates" (Andrew Chen)
- Banner ads started at 75%+ CTR → plummeted when commonplace
- Must constantly discover untapped strategies

### Using Other Channels to Feed Your Core

"You do not want to focus separately on multiple traction channels. However, you can utilize other channels as part of your core channel strategy."

Example: Core = content marketing (blog)
- Use targeting blogs for guest posts → feeds content marketing
- Buy social ads to amplify best posts → feeds content marketing
- Use community building, speaking, or offline ads to feed content marketing

Brainstorm how all 18 other channels could support your core channel.

### Online Tools and Quantification

**Sean Ellis (Dropbox/Eventbrite):** "The faster you run high-quality experiments, the more likely you'll find scalable, effective growth tactics."

- Use spreadsheet to rank and prioritize channel strategies
- Include: customers available, conversion rate, CPA, LTV
- Universal metrics allow cross-strategy comparison
- Calculate: "How many new customers do I need to move the needle?"
- If no reasonable chance a strategy yields enough customers within budget → skip it

## [TRC:CP] Critical Path

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapter 5
**Core Principle:** The path to reaching your traction goal with the fewest number of steps. Enumerate milestones absolutely necessary to reach your goal. Everything else is off-path. "Critical Path is a framework to help you decide what NOT to do."

### Defining Your Traction Goal

- Must be an explicit, quantified goal you're working toward
- Should be significant enough that hitting it would change your company's outcome
- Examples:
  - DuckDuckGo: 1% of general search market (meaningful because market is huge and few competitors)
  - DuckDuckGo earlier: 100M searches/month (break-even point)
  - DuckDuckGo even earlier: get product/messaging to point where people switch as primary search engine (Phase I → II)

**Working backward from traction goal:**
1. Set quantitative, time-based subgoals (e.g., 1,000 customers by next quarter)
2. Place traction activities on same calendar as product development
3. Reassess after each milestone — original enumeration is often wrong

### Milestones on Critical Path

DuckDuckGo goal = 100M searches/month:
- Faster site ✓ (on path)
- More compelling mobile offering ✓ (on path)
- More broadcast TV coverage ✓ (on path)
- Images and auto-suggest ✗ (off path — users were forgiving enough at that scale)

But for next goal (1% of market):
- Images and auto-suggest ✓ (on path — more mainstream users are less forgiving)

### Anti-Patterns

- Focusing on things off Critical Path (most common mistake)
- Not reassessing after milestones (market feedback changes what's needed)
- Spending resources on "nice to have" features that don't advance traction goal

## [TRC:BIAS] Overcoming Traction Biases

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapter 5
**Core Principle:** Three reasons founders ignore profitable channels: (1) out of sight/out of mind, (2) negative perception of certain channels, (3) bias against schlep (annoying/time-consuming). Getting a competitive advantage by acquiring customers in ways competitors refuse to try.

### Three Types of Traction Bias

1. **Unfamiliarity bias** — "Startups generally don't think of things like speaking engagements because they are usually out of their field of vision."
2. **Negative perception bias** — "Just because you hate talking on the phone doesn't mean your customers do." Channels like sales, affiliate marketing.
3. **Schlep bias** — Business development, trade shows seem annoying and time-consuming. But competitors avoid them too = competitive advantage.

### Jason Cohen's Insight

"I'll bet you a lot of your competition will refuse to even try these channels. And if that's true, that's even more reason to go try those channels! It can almost be a competitive advantage (at least a temporary one) if you can acquire customers in channels that others cannot, or refuse to try."

### How to Counteract

- Bullseye forces consideration of all 19 channels
- Good mentors help brainstorm and rank ideas
- Educate yourself about unfamiliar channels (this book exists for that purpose)
- Be honest: which channels are you biased for or against?
- The most underutilized channels in an industry are often the most promising

## [TRC:PIV] Pivoting and Traction

**Source:** Traction (Weinberg/Mares), Chapter 2
**Core Principle:** Many startups give up too early. Before pivoting, look for bright spots — pockets of true engagement. Being early to market is different from being in the wrong market. Sustainable product engagement growth is hard for any investor to ignore, even at small absolute numbers.

### When to Keep Going

- **Bright spots exist** — Some customers truly engaged with product. Examine why. Thread that unites them. Early adopters in huge market or outliers?
- **Early to market** — DuckDuckGo: privacy differentiator from 2009, didn't become mainstream issue until NSA leaks in 2013. Use early time to improve product.
- **Sustainable growth rate** — Even 100 customers growing 10% monthly for 6 months is attractive to investors.

### When to Pivot

- No evidence of real product engagement (no bright spots at all)
- Traction tests across multiple channels yield nothing promising
- Repeated Bullseye iterations with no results → product may need more tweaking ("bucket is still too leaky")

### Timescale Reality

"Are you ready to potentially do this for the next decade? In retrospect, a lot of founders feel they picked their company idea too quickly."
