## [MDW:ASK] The Ask Muscle
"No ASK? No GET." Kagan's #1 finding across 10,000+ Monthly1K students: the thing that held people back was **not** strategy — it was **Ask Avoidance**. "Getting money is not a matter of literally getting it. It's a matter of RECEIVING IT, which can happen only after one asks for it." Developing the Ask muscle is a **requirement**, not an option.

- The upside of asking is unlimited; the downside is minimal ("So they said no. Who cares!").
- Kagan gets scared and sad every time — "Every day, I feel the sting of rejection. And because of that, I succeed." (He cold-emailed a designer 6-8 hrs/day and got "Ha Ha Ha. You really think I would leave Google for your shitty company?!?")
- "Selling is helping" — if your product improves lives, sales is just education. Zig Ziglar: *"If you believe your product or service can fulfill a true need, it's your moral obligation to sell it."*

### [MDW:ASK.1] Rejection Goals
Kagan's copier-salesman father: *"Love rejections! Collect them like treasure! Set rejection goals. I shoot for a hundred rejections each week, because if you work that hard to get so many noes… you will find a few yeses, too."* Reframe rejection as **desirable** — aim *for* it, so you feel good when you get it.
- "This is going to suck. Let me aim to get at least twenty-five rejections." Turns it into a game vs. a blow to self-worth.
- Desensitize by repeated exposure; use discomfort as your compass.
- Sara Blakely's father asked nightly: **"What did you guys fail at this week?"** — conditioning that carried her through 7 years of door-to-door fax-machine rejection to become the youngest self-made female billionaire at 41.
- "You could be eleven noes away from your first million, but if you stop at the tenth rejection, you will have failed."

### [MDW:ASK.2] The Coffee Challenge
Go to any coffee shop (or any in-person spot), make a simple purchase, and **ask for 10% off. Say nothing else.** The whole point is to feel uncomfortable and practice asking. Over 10,000 people have done it; it's "the most powerful tool I've ever seen for improving your Ask muscle."

Exact script:
```
YOU:  Hi, how's it going?
THEM: Great, what would you like?
YOU:  I'd like a skinny low-fat vanilla latte.
THEM: Sure. That'll be $3.50.
YOU:  Can I get 10 percent off?
      [Key: state it clearly, with a smile, and say NOTHING afterwards.]
THEM: What's this for?
YOU:  I'm taking a business course and this is one of the assignments. :)
```
Worst case is trivial (a "no," a weird look). Brother Seth did it at Panera, got a "no," and "felt proud of himself afterwards." "Asking is a muscle, and this challenge is the gym." Start small, increase slowly; short-term games of rejection beat long-term fear.

### [MDW:ASK.3] Persistence & Follow-Up
- "Almost every no you get can eventually become a yes. Persistence will reveal that most noes are actually a **'not now.'**" Kagan wanted a Microsoft internship, was told there wasn't one for businesspeople, and followed up until "after the twelfth follow-up" she said, "Actually, we have an internship for businesspeople."
- **"Follow Up! Follow Up! Follow Up!"** Studies show a follow-up ask after an initial no is **TWICE as likely** to get a yes. At AppSumo, **~50% of sales come from follow-up emails.** Tools: followup.cc, Siri, Gmail Snooze, or just write it down.
- Kyle MacDonald turned **one red paperclip into a house with 14 asks.**

### [MDW:ASK.4] Chutzpah — Ask for Anything
Kagan's father sold copiers in broken English through sheer **chutzpah** (moxie, nerve). "Most people don't ask for what they want. They wish for it, they drop hints, they hope." Only asking gets you what you want — a job, a raise, a sale, better treatment at home. Fourth-grade Kagan went door-to-door selling magazine subscriptions and won the pizza-party contest by a blowout — "From that point forward I became an asking machine." College discount card (Ninja/HFG): he and intern Kenny walked into town and asked ~20 shops to offer discounts ("This is going to get your name in front of hundreds if not thousands of students") → **$50,000 within a year** ($10 cards costing $0.50 to print).

## Verbatim Ask & Pitch Scripts

These are the exact words from the book. Prefer them over paraphrase when the user needs a script.

### Ask a friend for a business idea (NOW, Not How Challenge)
```
Hey [first name], I'm trying to come up with some business ideas right now.

You know me well, so I was wondering what kind of business you think I'd
be good at?

[your name]
```

### Cold email #1 — ask a founder for a partnership (Kagan → Imgur's Alan Schaaf)
Landed AppSumo's first deal. Note the specific promise and the tiny, easy ask (a Friday chat):
```
SUBJECT: Promoting Imgur on Reddit

Hey Alan

Huge fan of Imgur and love using your product all the time.

We are launching a deal site and wanted to promote your Imgur Pro.

Think we can sell 200+ of it for you at no cost or work for you.

You free Friday to chat on AIM at 5pm PT?

Noah Kagan
```
"He jumped at the idea because, well, I promised him money at no cost!"

### Cold email #2 — ask for free advertising (Kagan → Reddit's Chris Slowe)
Asked for free ads, not advice, not a discount. Leads with a warm connection + genuine praise + a specific, low-friction ask:
```
SUBJECT: Hey Chris—friends with Chris Smoak

Hey Chris

Talked with Chris Smoak and he says hi. I LOVE what you've built with
Reddit. Huge user!

Wanted to treat you to a breakfast at Pork Store Cafe to give you some
suggestions on the site and run by a cool promotion I'm working on with
Imgur.

You free this Wed at 9am?

Be epic,
Noah Kagan
```
Result: "Why not? Our users love Imgur." (AppSumo was then built in 48 hours for $50 — a $12/hr Pakistani dev did 4 hours of PayPal-button work; first sale arrived within minutes; 200 licenses sold; a company doing $65M+ ten years later.)

## [MDW:SYS.4] Networking Asks — the same muscle, aimed at people
"Ninety percent of my net worth comes from meeting people… There's no such thing as self-made. Everyone is team-made." The Ask muscle builds your network too.

### Connect with a Prefluencer (compliment, no ask attached)
Reach ambitious people **before** they make it — easier to connect, and relationships compound. Kagan met Tim Ferriss (pre-*4-Hour Workweek*), Ramit Sethi (in college, blog making $0), and Harry Dry (Marketing Examples) this way. Three filters: (1) Whose work impresses you? (2) Who lacks a ton of attention and is likely to reply? (3) What can you do to help them? The message is a pure compliment — "Spam is asking for something; connecting is a compliment with no expectations":
```
Hey [first name],
LOVING what you're putting out. [Insert specifically what you liked or
how it impacted your life]
Keep going!
[Your Name]
```

### Andrew Chen's VIP referral ask (thank-you + ask for intros)
Andrew Chen's rule when new to the Bay Area: **"Meet five new people per day for my first six months."** In under a year he was connected to Marc Andreessen and Eric Yuan. After every meeting he sent a thank-you with highlights, to-dos, and a **specific ask for 1-2 intros**:
```
Hi Noah,
Quick thanks for meeting with me. You = awesome.
Here are 3 epic lessons I took away from our chat:
- [specific lesson 1]
- [specific quote they mentioned]
- [companies/people to watch they named]
Super grateful.
I'm curious: Are there 1–2 other people you believe I should meet?
Thanks again, Andrew
```
His cold intro email always carried three things — a short blurb about himself, the **value he offers** (what's in it for them), and why he's excited to meet — plus a mutual-connection hook in the subject line and social proof in a P.S. "Telling someone why you are interesting, how you can help, and why you want to meet works like gangbusters."

**Advisor reminder (Scope Disclaimer 2):** MDW's bar is real money up front — enforce it. But these ask-scripts do not replace *problem discovery*. If the user needs to learn **why** a customer would pay (not just whether they'll bite on a warm ask), route to The Mom Test; MDW's asks are best aimed at your existing Zone of Influence, who are predisposed to say yes.
