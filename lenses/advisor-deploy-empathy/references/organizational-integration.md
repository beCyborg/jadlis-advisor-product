# Organizational Integration — Making Research Actionable & Continuous

Source: Deploy Empathy (Michele Hansen, 2021), Parts IV, V, X, Appendix B
Citation prefix: `[DE:OI]`

## Core Principle: Research Embedded in Everything

Customer research — не one-off event, а постоянная часть принятия решений. Stripe: "Who are the users, and what do they care about?" — первый вопрос при любом product proposal. `[DE:OI.1]`

---

## Stripe Model — Gold Standard `[DE:OI.1]`

### Culture

- Founders (Patrick & John Collison) лично отвечали на support emails и смотрели как users интегрируют API в ранние дни
- "Baked into Stripe's DNA" — research with users from day one
- New employees: first thing = try to answer a support ticket
- "You're expected to talk to users throughout your time at Stripe, regardless of function"
- Developers, PMs, designers — ALL interview customers directly

### Targeted Research Process (Stripe)

1. Write specific problem hypothesis or question
2. Kickoff with ALL stakeholders (eng, design, product, marketing, sales, pricing) + dedicated researcher if available
3. Define characteristics of target users
4. Brainstorm ALL questions that answer the problem
5. Curate: remove duplicates and leading questions
6. Workshop questions internally for flow and relevancy
7. Pull user list (customers OR prospects/competitor users)
8. Reach out
9. Interview

**Result:** Script that "almost anyone can run with and go talk to users and get high-quality signal."

### Ongoing Research (Stripe)

Teams send emails every few months to recent users:
> "Hey, we saw you just did [X]. If you're open to it, we'd love to hop on a call and learn more about what went well, what didn't, and how we can do better."

- All team members participate (devs + PMs)
- Feeds into targeted research as themes emerge
- Keeps "pulse" on user experience

## Two Types of Research `[DE:OI.2]`

### Project-Based Research `[DE:OI.PR]`

**Definition:** Narrowly defined question, defined goal, defined timeline.

**Examples:**
- "Why aren't people buying from this landing page?"
- "How to reduce support tickets about [X]?"
- "Should we launch a new product for [problem]?"
- "What do people think of this prototype?"

**Hansen/Geocodio Process:**
1. Question emerges
2. Look at existing data (analytics, support history, surveys, competitors)
3. Get quantitative picture — is problem big enough to warrant investigation?
4. Set up interviews: 5-10 customers over 2 weeks (phone/screen share)
5. Brainstorm and wireframe/prototype solutions
6. Go back to SAME customers for further feedback
7. Total cycle: 1-2 months, concurrent with other work

### Ongoing Research `[DE:OI.OR]`

**Definition:** Daily/weekly building of customer understanding.

**Hansen methods:**
1. **Short surveys** — 1-2 questions in product (NPS-style + open-ended)
2. **New customer interviews** — 1-3 months after signup
3. **Happy customer interviews** — top 80% revenue
4. **Canceled customer interviews** — the most challenging

## Loss Aversion Warning `[DE:OI.3]`

### Why Companies Over-Focus on Churned Customers

Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky's loss aversion: people would rather avoid losing money than gaining same amount.

**Business symptom:** Spending more time on cancellation prevention than on understanding happy customers.

**Hansen's counter-argument:**
- Happy customer interviews = preemptive cancellation interviews
- More you understand well-served use cases → more you attract good-fit customers → fewer cancellations
- Happy customers become advocates, which attracts MORE good-fit customers
- Focus on happy > Focus on churned (for long-term health)

**Decision:** Balance happy and canceled interviews. If you must choose, start with happy.

## Recruiting Participants `[DE:RC]`

### Reddit Method `[DE:RC.1]`

**Best for:** Pre-product, no customers yet

**Process:**
1. Determine WHO you want to talk to (be specific — virtual assistants, not "professionals")
2. Research subreddits (there are subreddits for EVERYTHING including professional fields)
3. Check rules — is recruiting post allowed?
4. Write post:
   - You're looking for research help
   - Describe problem (without biasing toward your solution)
   - Who you're looking for
   - What they'll do
   - How to contact you
   - [Optional: incentive]
5. Sort through applicants → schedule calls with 7-8 (expect some no-shows)
6. Conduct calls
7. Send gift card WHILE on phone at end
8. Ask for confirming comment on post
9. Send thank-you note next day

**Template:**
> *Looking for people to talk to about [problem] — $10 Amazon gift card!*
> *I'm looking to talk to [specific role] about [process].*
> *I'm a [role] and looking to understand [what].*
> *30-minute phone call, 5 people, next 2 weeks.*
> *All calls confidential. DM me if interested.*

**Hansen's HealthIT example:** 5,000 member subreddit → ~70 replies → whittled to best fits.

### Twitter Method `[DE:RC.2]`

**Best for:** Finding people already talking about problem/competitor

**Find people who:**
- Negatively post about competitor
- Post about the process/problem
- Blogged about relevant negative experiences

**Template (no product):**
> "Hey, I'm thinking about building something related to [thing you posted about]. I noticed you were unhappy with [competitor]. (Link) Any chance you'd be willing to have a call?"

**Template (have product):**
> "Hey, I notice you're frustrated with [X], and my [thing] aims to make that easier. (Link) Any chance you'd take a look? (Not trying to sell, just want feedback.)"

### LinkedIn Method `[DE:RC.3]`

**Best for:** B2B, professional audiences

**Key:** Appeal to desire to be helpful. Research on person BEFORE messaging. Truly personalized = critical.

**Chris Forster template (most successful):**
> "Hi [Name], I'm doing research as a tech startup founder on how innovative publishers are reaching new audiences. I'd love to talk about this if you have 10 mins?"

**Note "innovative publishers"** — complimentary framing increases response rate.

### Email Method (Existing Customers) `[DE:RC.4]`

**Formula:**
- Who you are (title matters — founder, product manager)
- Specific amount of time
- Why you want to talk to THEM
- When (calendar link)
- What they get (feature input usually enough incentive)

**Template:**
> *Hi, I'm the founder of [company] and I notice you [recently started/have been using/used feature].*
> *I'm hoping you might have 20-30 minutes to tell me about how our product helps you.*
> *Your feedback will directly influence our roadmap.*
> *Pick a time: [calendar link]*

**Targeting filters:**
- Time-based (signed up last 30 days, customer >1 year)
- Revenue-based (specific plan, high LTV, free trial converts)
- Behavior-based (used feature, didn't use feature)

**PS trick:** "And yes, this is an automated email, but replies come back to me directly and I'll personally reply."

**Response rate benchmark:** 5-10% is very good. This is a volume game.

## Sales ≠ Support ≠ Research `[DE:OI.4]`

### Critical Distinction

| Activity | Goal | Approach | Outcome |
|----------|------|----------|---------|
| **Sales** | Close deal | Present value, handle objections | Revenue |
| **Support** | Solve immediate problem | Fix, educate, retain | Resolution |
| **Research** | Understand WHY | Listen, don't solve | Insights |

**Common mistake:** Treating support conversations as research. Support asks "How can I fix this?" Research asks "Can you walk me through what happened?"

**Another mistake:** Selling on research calls. NEVER try to sell during interview. If they want to buy afterwards → great. But don't force it.

## Feature Requests as Research `[DE:OI.5]`

### Feature Request → Interview Opportunity

**Phone template:**
> "Thank you for suggesting that! I'm curious, can you tell me a bit more about what you're trying to do overall? I'd love to understand how that feature would fit into your process."

**Text template:**
> "Thank you for the suggestion! I'm wondering if you could tell me a little more about what you're trying to accomplish overall and what's prompted this idea?"

### Cataloguing Feature Requests

Don't just list features — capture:
- What problem they're trying to solve (the WHY behind the request)
- How they currently work around it
- How often they encounter it
- Who else is involved
- Impact if unsolved

## Research for Solo Founders `[DE:OI.6]`

### Hansen's Experience (Geocodio)

Two-person team → $1M+ annual revenue. Secret: "listening to customers is embedded in everything we do."

**Solo founder advantages:**
- Shorter feedback loops (no committee)
- Can pivot faster based on insights
- Every interaction = research opportunity (support, sales, casual)

**Solo founder challenges:**
- No one to bounce ideas off → practice interviews help
- Time constraints → even 1 interview/month = more than zero
- Emotional toll → limit to 1-2 per day max

### Minimum Viable Research for Solo Founders

1. **Week 1:** One-question survey in product ("What did you use before?")
2. **Week 2-3:** Reply to interesting survey answers, offer to chat
3. **Week 3-4:** 2-3 interviews (discovery or switch)
4. **Monthly:** One ongoing interview (happy customer)
5. **Quarterly:** Review patterns, adjust product/marketing

## Continuous Discovery Habits `[DE:OI.7]`

### Integration Checklist

- [ ] Short survey always running in product
- [ ] Reply to ALL survey open-ended responses
- [ ] Interview at least 1 person/month (minimum)
- [ ] New customer interviews: 1-3 months after signup
- [ ] Happy customer interviews: quarterly sample from top revenue
- [ ] Cancellation interviews: whenever relevant (after comfort with other types)
- [ ] Feature requests: always ask WHY + process context
- [ ] Support interactions: note patterns, themes for project research
- [ ] Landing pages as research: test messaging, measure response
- [ ] All team members participate (if team exists)

### "Evaluate, Not Validate" `[DE:OI.8]`

Adam Amran: "'validating' an idea smells of confirmation bias... 'evaluating' would prime us to be more open to new insights."

Stripe PM Theodora Chu: "It's important to make sure you're using research as an effective tool and not just using it as a way to say you checked the box... if you were going to make a decision regardless [of what users said], then you're wasting their time."

**Test:** Before interview, ask yourself: "Am I willing to be wrong?" If no → don't interview yet, adjust mindset first.

## Participant Diversity `[DE:OI.9]`

### Why It Matters (Derek Featherstone)

Including disabled people in research:
1. Some share pain points with non-disabled people (validates broader patterns)
2. New pain points emerge you weren't aware of (new opportunities)

**Example:** Research showed podcast consumption = mobile + on the go. But missed Deaf/hard-of-hearing users who prefer computer + transcripts.

**Leaving out groups = leaving opportunities on the table.**

### Practical Steps
- Mix demographics in recruitment
- Include different company sizes
- Include different experience levels
- Include people with disabilities
- Notice if responses cluster — might be missing segments

## Neuroscience of Listening `[DE:OI.10]`

### Why Interviews Build Loyalty

fMRI brain studies: parts associated with motivation, reward, enjoyment light up when people talk about themselves to another person.

**Implication:** Being listened to makes people happy → positive feelings transferred to you and your company.

> "Just listening to customers alone has benefits for you and your company. Even if you do nothing with what you've learned afterward."

Hansen's observation: "Over thousands of interviews, customers we interview tend to become our most vocal supporters. They become the ones who passionately share the word. They are most likely to offer testimonials, even without asking."

**Bottom line:** Even imperfect interviews where you're still learning → better than no interviews. The ACT of listening creates value.
