# Business Models для Company of One

Source: Company of One (Paul Jarvis, 2019), Chapters 8-11
Citation prefix: `[CO1:BM]`

## Core Principle: Scalable Systems, Not Scalable Teams

Масштабируй системы (email, automation, outsourcing), а не headcount. Revenue может расти без пропорционального роста расходов и сотрудников. `[CO1:BM.1]`

## MVPr — Minimum Viable Profit `[CO1:BM.2]`

### Decision Algorithm

**APPLY WHEN:** Запуск нового продукта или бизнеса
**AVOID WHEN:** У тебя уже стабильный profitable бизнес

**Формула:**
- MVPr = минимальная прибыль, покрывающая зарплату основателю
- Чем ниже number → тем быстрее достигнешь → тем скорее начнёшь УЧИТЬСЯ
- Прибыль > расходы = жизнеспособность; стабильная прибыль = viability

**Алгоритм достижения MVPr:**
1. Определи personal cost of living (минимум)
2. Скейли DOWN: меньше фич, меньше сотрудников, меньше overhead
3. Запускай FAST: первая версия ≠ финальная
4. Итерируй на основе DATA, не assumptions
5. Profit first, growth second — никогда наоборот

### Key Insight: Focus vs Growth
Фокус на profit и фокус на growth — почти невозможно одновременно. Growth требует investment с надеждой на future return. Profit = reinvest from realized gains.

## Launch & Iterate `[CO1:BM.3]`

### "Launch Quickly, Launch Often"

> "If you aren't embarrassed by the first version of your product, you've launched too late." — Reid Hoffman

**Examples:**
- Groupon: basic website + manual PDFs в Apple Mail
- Pebble: один explainer video + Kickstarter → $20M
- Virgin: один Boeing 747, один маршрут
- WD-40: буквально 39 провалов и 1 успех
- Crew: одностраничный сайт + форма → ручной matching freelancers

**Launch Process для Company of One:**
1. Simple in choice — один продукт, одна проблема
2. Simple in messaging — Casper: "better sleep", не "108 mattress types"
3. Simple in targeting — один hyper-specific audience segment
4. Three elements (Harvard, George Whitesides): Predictable + Accessible + Building Block

### Service-First Strategy `[CO1:BM.3.1]`

Перед созданием продукта — продай как сервис:
- Danielle LaPorte: "Fire Starter Sessions" consulting → product launch → $1M+ business
- Halley Gray (Evolve + Succeed): service first → prove market → then productize
- Результат: revenue с day 1, market validation, audience intel

## Teaching as Marketing `[CO1:BM.4]`

### Brian Clark / CopyBlogger Model

Brian Clark: lawyer → real estate (educational content → clients) → CopyBlogger ($12M+ revenue, 200K+ customers).

**Почему teaching > selling:**
1. **Domain authority** — если учишь, тебя воспринимают как эксперта `[CO1:TE.1]`
2. **Show benefits** — электромобиль: учи о экономии $, а не о характеристиках `[CO1:TE.2]`
3. **Customer success** — образованные клиенты дольше остаются `[CO1:TE.3]`
4. **Transparency builds trust** — идеи без execution = бесполезны, делись свободно `[CO1:TE.4]`

### MIT Sloan Research (Eisingerich & Bell)
1,200 клиентов инвестиционной фирмы: чем больше education → тем больше trust → тем больше loyalty → тем выше appreciation for customer service.

### Casper Model — Education as Brand
- "Van Winkles" и "Pillow Talk" — публикации о науке сна, НЕ о матрасах
- Нет рекламных ссылок в контенте
- 100-night trial + better sleep education → market share grows without retail stores

### Greg Berns Neuroscience (Emory, 2009)
Decision-making центры мозга ЗАМЕДЛЯЮТСЯ когда получают advice от trusted expert. Customers consistently rate experts как most trusted spokespeople, выше CEO и celebrities.

## Trust & Scale `[CO1:BM.5]`

### Three Aspects of Trust (Glen Urban, MIT)

| Аспект | Определение | Пример |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Confidence** | "Я верю, что ты говоришь правду" | Transparent pricing, honest reviews |
| **Competence** | "Я верю, что ты умеешь это делать" | Teaching, authority, track record |
| **Benevolence** | "Я верю, что ты действуешь в моих интересах" | Fiduciary duty, customer-first decisions |

### Trust by Proxy = Word of Mouth `[CO1:TS.2]`

**Data:**
- Nielsen: 92% consumers trust recommendations from family/friends
- Word of Mouth Marketing Association: WOM drives 5x more sales than paid media, $6T annually
- Texas Tech: 83% willing to refer, only 29% actually do → MASSIVE missed opportunity
- SmallBizTrends: 83% new business from WOM referrals

**Actionable Referral System:**
1. Week after purchase → automated email: "How would you rate (1-10)?"
2. Score >7 → second email with double-sided incentive + pre-written share text
3. Follow-up with clients 2-4 weeks after project → collect testimonials + ask for referrals
4. Reward loyalty: MailChimp swag strategy (T-shirts without logo → social media posts → brand awareness)

### Niche Trust `[CO1:TS.3]`

Kurt Elster: general ecommerce consultant → Shopify-only consultant → revenue 8x, featured on Shopify site, speaking gigs worldwide.

**Paradox of specificity:** чем уже ниша → тем легче продавать → тем больше premium можно ставить.

## Scalable Systems `[CO1:BM.6]`

### Connection — Email Marketing as 1-to-Many

- Average ROI email marketing: 3,800% (Data & Marketing Association)
- Paul Jarvis: 93% revenue через email, 30K list
- Campaign Monitor: personalized subject lines = 26% more opens
- Epsilon: segmented automation = 70.5% higher open rate, 152% higher CTR

**Email Automation Playbook:**
1. Welcome sequence → education + product value
2. Post-purchase → how to use + success stories
3. Follow-up after time → check-in + upsell
4. Segmented pitches → only to non-buyers

### Creation — Outsource Manufacturing `[CO1:BM.6.1]`

**Need/Want Model** (~$10M revenue, <10 employees):
- Shopify for store (scales from 1 to 1M+ orders)
- No retail → no sales team
- Outsourced manufacturing + shipping/fulfillment
- Marketing: 3 people, all online (social, ads, email)
- Direct-to-consumer = lean, experimental, fast

### Collaboration — Scale DOWN `[CO1:BM.6.2]`

**Counterintuitive:** collaboration = единственная область где company of one должен УМЕНЬШАТЬ масштаб.
- Slack/messaging = "asyncronish" distractions, NOT focused work
- Basecamp/Buffer: disconnect most of the day, responses expected in DAYS not minutes
- Hackathons = focused collaboration (good) vs 24/7 availability (bad)

## Funding Without VC `[CO1:BM.7]`

### Crowdfunding Model

**Ugmonk/Gather:** Jeff Sheldon → Kickstarter → $430K (2,394% of goal) → funded by existing audience, no investors.

**Crowdfunding vs VC Decision:**

| Factor | Crowdfunding | VC |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| Control | Full | Partial (board seats, voting rights) |
| Validation | Market tests idea directly | Investor tests YOUR pitch |
| Timeline | Campaign = weeks | Fundraising = months |
| Gender gap | Women 32% more successful (PwC) | Predominantly white male investors prefer similar founders (HBS) |
| Scale | Consumer-facing products (Yale) | Works for B2B and capital-intensive |
| Success rate | ~35% Kickstarter funded | Very low (most pitches rejected) |
| Mentorship | Minimal | Often included |

### Bootstrap Model

**Derek Sivers / CDBaby:**
- Started by selling own band's CDs → friends asked → business born
- $500 to start → $300 first month → $700 second → profitable forever
- Sold for $22M in 2008 ($250K/month net profit at time)
- Never took investors despite weekly offers
- "Every decision should be what's best for customers"

## Personality as Competitive Advantage `[CO1:BM.8]`

### Pistachio Ice Cream Strategy `[CO1:PP.2]`

Don't be vanilla (universally acceptable but bland). Be pistachio (some love it, some hate it, but those who love it pay premium).

**Three polarization strategies:**
1. **Placation** — change haters' minds (General Mills: low-carb/gluten-free mixes)
2. **Prodding** — antagonize haters to rally neutrals (WestJet vs United: "#OwnersCare")
3. **Amplification** — double down on unique trait (Marmite XO: extra-strength version → 50K website visits, 300K Facebook views, sold out)

### Decision: When to Polarize

- **APPLY WHEN:** You have clear values, specific audience, unique personality trait
- **AVOID WHEN:** You haven't defined your audience, your product isn't differentiated
- **Derek Sivers rule:** "Proudly exclude people" → message resonates stronger with target

### Customer Happiness = Marketing `[CO1:CS.1]`

**Data:**
- Harris Interactive: 9/10 Americans spend more with great customer service
- 79% bailed on transaction due to poor service
- White House: loyal customers worth 10x first purchase
- Ruby Newell-Legner: only 4% voice dissatisfaction; 91% just leave
- Nottingham University: apologies often work BETTER than financial compensation
- Acquisition cost: 5-7x more than retention (Econsultancy)

**Customer Success Framework:**
1. Treat every customer like your ONLY customer
2. Own ALL mistakes (even vendor's) — take personal responsibility
3. Exceed expectations, don't just meet norms
4. Build reciprocity through unexpected touches (RackSpace pizza story)
5. Create customer success department / focus (SalesForce Pardot: largest dept → #1 Forbes innovative company)
