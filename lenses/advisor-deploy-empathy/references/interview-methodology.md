# Interview Methodology — Empathetic Interview Structure & Tactics

Source: Deploy Empathy (Michele Hansen, 2021), Parts III, VI, VII
Citation prefix: `[DE:IM]`

## Core Principle: Create a Bubble of Suspended Judgment

Цель customer interview — создать пространство, где человек чувствует абсолютную безопасность и открытость. Интервьюер говорит 10%, клиент 90%. `[DE:IM.1]`

> "Empathy is about understanding how another person thinks, and acknowledging their reasoning and emotions as valid, even if they differ from your own understanding." — Indi Young

## 12 Tactics: How to Talk So People Will Talk `[DE:IM.3]`

### Tactic 1: Gentle Tone of Voice `[DE:IM.GT]`

**Что делать:** "Late-night DJ voice" (Chris Voss). Мягкий, спокойный, without judgment голос. Как если бы спрашивал пожилого родственника о старой фотографии.

**НЕ делать:** Condescending tone. НЕ говори как с ребёнком — говори как с уважаемым учителем.

**Пример трансформации:**
- BAD: "Why did you do it that way?" (medium-volume, emphasis = accusatory)
- GOOD: "What led you to do it like that?" (gentle, curious voice)

### Tactic 2: Validate Them `[DE:IM.VA]`

**Что делать:** Validating statements показывают engagement БЕЗ agreement/disagreement.

**Ключевые фразы:**
- "That makes sense."
- "I can see why you'd do it that way."
- "It sounds like that's frustrating/time consuming/challenging."
- "Can you help me understand what went through your mind when [X]?"
- "It sounds like a lot goes into that."
- "I can see what you're saying."

**CRITICAL:** Используй "think" вместо "feel" — "You think the process is complicated" (neutral) vs "You feel..." (can be insulting).

**НЕ делать:** "Yeah, I agree!" / "That's great!" / "You're right!" — любое opinion breaks the bubble.

**Quesadilla story:** 45 минут в интервью, человек говорит "Sorry, I'm eating a quesadilla." Ответ Dr. Helen Fake: "Oh, you're fine!" — не "No worries" (negating), не "Don't worry about it" (invalidating), а explicitly валидирует ЕГО состояние.

### Tactic 3: Leave Pauses `[DE:IM.PA]`

**Terry Gross Rule:** Задай вопрос → жди минимум 3 beat-а, пока не станет uncomfortable. "The other person will fill the silence, and what they fill it with will often be the most interesting part of the interview."

**НЕ делать:** Prompting — "Was it because of X, or maybe Y?" Задай вопрос и МОЛЧИ.

**Если молчание затягивается:**
- "Yes, I was just giving you a moment to think."
- "Oh, I was just jotting down what you said. That seemed important. [Mirror phrase]"
- "Oh, it just sounded like you were about to say something."

**Regional differences (Dr. Deborah Tannen):** Northeastern US = talk over each other (engagement). California = wait for pause. East Asia = even longer pauses. Adjust your 3 beats accordingly.

### Tactic 4: Mirror and Summarize `[DE:IM.MI]`

**Mirroring** = повтори что сказали. **Summarizing** = перефразируй + label feelings.

**Example (Drew Firebase interview):**
- Drew: "...running into a lot of walls, jumping through a lot of hoops..."
- Michele: "Can you tell me a little bit more about those hoops and walls that you ran into?"

**Key:** Используй "It sounds like..." (centers situation) вместо "I'm hearing that..." (centers YOU). Chris Voss principle.

**Follow-ups are NOT questions.** Restate as statement → prompts elaboration:
- Drew describes frustration → Michele: "It sounds like you had a lot of things you were trying to weigh..." → Drew elaborates further.

### Tactic 5: Don't Interrupt `[DE:IM.NI]`

> "Contrary to popular opinion, listening is not a passive activity. It is the most active thing you can do." — Chris Voss

**Practice:** Проведи 1-2 недели сознательно не прерывая людей. Замечай когда хочешь прервать.

**Allowed responses:** Mhmm / Can you say more? / (Mirroring) / (Nothing)

### Tactic 6: Use Simple Wording `[DE:IM.SW]`

- BAD: "What are your objectives?"
- GOOD: "So, can you give me the big picture of what you're trying to do?"

Не беспокойся о grammar. Verbal fillers OK. Don't use jargon unless THEY use it first.

### Tactic 7: Ask for Clarification (Even When Not Needed) `[DE:IM.CL]`

**Standard:** "Can I just make sure I understand? First you [step 1], then [step 2]... Does that sound right?"

**Advanced — Intentionally Misstate:** Slightly change order/detail → interviewee corrects and OVEREXPLAINS → incredibly detailed info.

### Tactic 8: Don't Explain or Get Defensive `[DE:IM.NE]`

When someone criticizes your product:
- BAD: "Well, the reason it works that way is..."
- GOOD: "Can you tell me how you expected it to work?"
- GOOD: "What were you hoping to use this for?"

Negative feedback = compliment (they care enough to tell you).

### Tactic 9: Build on What They Say `[DE:IM.BO]`

Improv rule: "Yes, and..." НЕ "No, but..." / "Well, actually..."

> "In improv, you have to roll with whatever the other person does and build on what they do, otherwise the rhythm will be lost." — Rocky Gray

### Tactic 10: Let Them Be Expert `[DE:IM.ER]`

**Everyone is the expert of their own experience, even if it isn't factually correct.**

**Example:** Person says "I used Laravel for the frontend and Tailwind for the backend" (wrong). DON'T correct. Follow up: "What was it like to use Laravel for the frontend?"

**Price example:** Person says "$399" when they paid $299. Don't correct in interview. Email next day only if it materially matters to them.

**Wayne's store:** Hansen grew up calling store "Jack's" even though owner was Wayne. Not factually correct, but emotionally valid. Everyone deserves this grace.

### Tactic 11: Use Their Words and Pronunciation `[DE:IM.TW]`

If they say "Nev-AHH-da" instead of "Ne-VAD-ah" — USE THEIR pronunciation. If they say "MailMonkey" instead of MailChimp — say "MailMonkey."

Correction = reduces them to lesser role = shatters trust bubble.

### Tactic 12: Ask About Past/Current Behavior `[DE:IM.PB]`

**Golden Rule:** Ask about what people HAVE DONE, not what they WOULD DO.

**Useless questions:**
- "Would you use this?" (people are polite, can't predict future)
- "What are your pain points?" (offensive/vague)
- "What do you need?" (asks them to design your product)

**Powerful questions about behavior:**
- "How long does it take to do [X]?"
- "What tools do you use for [X]?"
- "How much do you pay for [those tools]?"
- "Can you tell me about the last time you did [X]?"
- "What did you use before you used [product]?"

**Signs of pain (without asking about pain):** Time spent, money spent, manual solutions, multiple tools, frustration markers in tone.

## Interview Scripts `[DE:SC]`

### Discovery / Customer-Problem Script `[DE:SC.1]`

**When:** Evaluating idea, no product yet or exploring new area

**Core questions (adapt, don't read verbatim):**
1. Can you tell me about the last time you did [X]?
2. What were you trying to do overall?
3. Can you walk me through the steps?
4. Where in that process do you spend the most time?
5. What tools do you use?
6. How much do you pay for [those tools]?
7. What do you think of them?
8. What have you already tried to solve this?
9. How often do you experience this?
10. If you could wave a magic wand, what would you change?

### Switch Script (New Customer) `[DE:SC.2]`

**When:** Understanding why new customers bought (1-3 months after signup)

**Focus:** Four Forces — push toward new, pull of new, anxiety about switching, inertia of existing.

### Long-time Customer Script `[DE:SC.3]`

**When:** Understanding why happy customers stay (top 80% revenue)

**Key insight:** Happy customer interviews = preemptive cancellation interviews.

### Cancellation Script `[DE:SC.4]`

**When:** Understanding why customers leave

**Warning:** Most challenging interviews. Do AFTER becoming comfortable with other types.

### Interactive Script (Prototype/Website) `[DE:SC.5]`

**When:** Testing something people can interact with

**Special rule:** Avoid word "confusing" — use "What did you expect to happen?"

### Card Sorting Script `[DE:SC.6]`

**When:** Feature prioritization

## Interview Preparation Checklist `[DE:IM.4]`

- [ ] Conduct over AUDIO (screen share only when necessary) — video = less openness
- [ ] Mute phone and notifications
- [ ] Set up focus environment
- [ ] Aim for 5 people; cycles of 5 with narrower scope if needed
- [ ] OK if interview doesn't follow exact question order
- [ ] Stick to promised time
- [ ] Always write thank-you note
- [ ] Incentive/gift only when appropriate (Reddit = yes, existing customers = usually no)
- [ ] Ask permission before recording
- [ ] Don't do more than 2 interviews per day
- [ ] Use appropriate formality
- [ ] NEVER sell on the call

## Debugging Interviews `[DE:IM.5]`

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| No-shows | Overbook by 2-3, send reminders |
| Short interviews | More follow-up questions, use pauses |
| Nervous person | Extra gentle tone, more validation |
| Short answers | Mirror + "Can you tell me more about that?" |
| Cagey person | Emphasize confidentiality, use validation |
| Feature request mode | "What were you hoping [feature] would help you do?" |
| Expected onboarding | Clarify purpose, pivot to discovery questions |
| Multiple people on call | Address each, ask who does what |
| Off-topic | "Thank you for telling me that. Can we go back to..." |
| Mad customer | Extra validation, acknowledge frustration, listen |

## Grocery Store Cashier Story `[DE:IM.6]`

Hansen's pivotal realization: using interview tactics unconsciously at grocery store → cashier shared detailed retirement planning in 5 minutes. Same cashier, years of small talk before.

**Lessons:**
1. Tactics weave into everyday conversation with practice
2. Be careful — people can share more than intended
3. "Back off or shift topics when someone is on the verge of sharing too much"
4. Many people don't have anyone who just listens to them about daily tasks/worries
5. "Just being a presence who is willing to listen is more powerful than people realize"

## Practice Before Real Interviews `[DE:IM.7]`

### Step-by-Step Learning Path

1. Read "How to Talk" tactics
2. Practice with friend/family: "What's something new you bought in last 3 months?"
3. Analyze practice: identify functional/emotional/social factors
4. Self-assess: which tactics came naturally, which need work
5. Interview ONE real person, adjust
6. Work up to 5 interviews
7. Analyze → research loop → repeat

### Rachel and the Shampoo Story

First-time interviewer Rachel discovered DEEP insight from "simple" shampoo purchase:
- Surface: grabbed it on sale at grocery store
- Discovered: beach trip weeks before → hair looked good with salt water → hope for volume (thin hair problem) → emotional memory of beach with friends → took chance on "Sea Minerals" shampoo
- All from ~15 minutes, zero prior experience

**Lesson:** Anyone can find hidden insights with the right technique. Practice makes comfortable.
