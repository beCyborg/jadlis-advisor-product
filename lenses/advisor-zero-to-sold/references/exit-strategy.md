## Building a Sellable Business `[ZTS:GR.1]`

### Core Philosophy: Built to Sell

**Source**: John Warrillow, "Built to Sell" — структурируй компанию для лёгкой продажи. Даже если никогда не продашь, получишь benefits.

**Core Principle**: Строй бизнес как будто собираешься его продать. Это делает его лучше для тебя прямо сейчас И привлекательным для покупателя в будущем.

**FeedbackPanda Example**: Читали "Built to Sell" до основания → строили как sellable с day one → продали менее чем через 2 года с mid-five-figure MRR → transition был "extremely painless".

### What Makes a Business Sellable `[ZTS:GR.1.1]`

**Decision Algorithm — Sellability Checklist**:

1. **Focused** `[ZTS:GR.1.2]`
   - One specialized service для clearly defined niche
   - Acquirer needs training only in one area
   - Pre-optimized through expert insights
   - Standardized offerings (NOT bespoke) → economies of scale
   - APPLY WHEN: Оцениваешь привлекательность бизнеса для покупателя
   - AVOID WHEN: Слишком рано сужать focus — на Preparation/early Survival stage разумно experiment

2. **Can Run Without You** `[ZTS:GR.1.3]`
   - Automation of repetitive tasks
   - Documentation of all processes (SOPs)
   - Employees/contractors for key functions
   - If founder still needed for daily ops → scary for buyers
   - FeedbackPanda: всё документировано → trained CS rep за дни при передаче

3. **Independent** `[ZTS:GR.1.4]`
   - Diverse customer base — no single customer > 10% revenue
   - No platform dependency that could destroy business overnight
   - Multiple revenue streams where possible
   - Acquirers will NOT buy a business that implodes if one customer cancels

4. **Does Not Have to Be Sold** `[ZTS:GR.1.5]`
   - Best part: you can keep it and watch value grow
   - Reject/defer acquisition offers until ready
   - Continuing growth after first inquiry → validates buyer's thesis → higher price later

## Valuation Framework `[ZTS:EX.2]`

### How Bootstrapped SaaS is Valued

**Core Principle**: Valuation is HIGHLY subjective. Same business = significantly different value between acquirers.

**Key Metrics Buyers Look At**:

| Metric | What It Means | Impact on Valuation |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| MRR/ARR | Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue | Primary driver |
| Growth Rate | MoM or YoY revenue growth | Higher growth = higher multiple |
| Churn Rate | Monthly cancellation rate | Low churn = predictable revenue = premium |
| LTV | Customer Lifetime Value | Higher LTV = more valuable customers |
| CAC | Customer Acquisition Cost | Lower CAC = more efficient business |
| LTV:CAC Ratio | Efficiency of growth | >3:1 is healthy |
| Net Revenue Retention | Revenue from existing customers | >100% = expansion revenue |

**Typical Multiples for Bootstrapped SaaS**: 3x-5x ARR для healthy businesses. Premium для:
- Low churn (<3% monthly)
- High growth (>20% YoY)
- Extensive automation and documentation
- Diverse customer base
- No founder dependency

### Revenue Quality Factors `[ZTS:EX.2.1]`

- **Recurring > one-time**: Subscription revenue = predictable = premium
- **Annual plans > monthly**: Annual commitments = lower churn = premium
- **Diversified > concentrated**: Many small customers > few whales
- **Organic > paid**: Organic growth (WOM, content) = sustainable = premium
- **Expanding > flat**: Net revenue retention >100% = customers spending more over time

## Finding Buyers `[ZTS:EX.3]`

### Types of Acquirers

1. **Strategic Acquirers** — Companies in adjacent space wanting to add your product/audience
   - Pay highest multiples
   - Want synergies with existing business
   - May want to acqui-hire your team

2. **Private Equity** — Financial buyers looking for cash-flowing businesses
   - FeedbackPanda was acquired by PE firm (SureSwift Capital)
   - Focus on returns and operational efficiency
   - Often do "roll-ups" — buy multiple similar businesses

3. **Individual Buyers** — Entrepreneurs wanting to run an established business
   - Usually smaller deals
   - May want to keep current team
   - Often found on marketplaces

4. **Competitors** — Buying market share or eliminating competition
   - Can pay premium for strategic value
   - Risk: may shut down your product
   - Consider impact on your customers

### Where Buyers Find You

- **IndieHackers**: Sharing revenue publicly → attracted first offers for FeedbackPanda
- **Microacquire/Acquire.com**: Marketplace for buying/selling small SaaS businesses
- **SaaS brokers**: FE International, Quiet Light, Empire Flippers
- **Direct outreach**: PE firms and strategic buyers reach out when they see growing metrics
- **Founder communities**: Conferences, Twitter, podcasts

### Attracting Acquisition Interest `[ZTS:EX.3.1]`

**Decision Algorithm**:
- Share Stripe-verified revenue on IndieHackers (enough to attract, not enough to enable copycats)
- Blog about your journey → creates narrative that buyers want to be part of
- Attend founder conferences → personal connections with PE firms
- Make your business discoverable: clear landing page, public metrics, contact info
- When offers come unsolicited → take them seriously, even if not ready

## Deal Structure `[ZTS:EX.4]`

### Common Terms

**Asset vs Stock Sale**: Most SaaS acquisitions are asset sales (buyer acquires product, code, customers, not the legal entity).

**Payment Structure**:
- **Cash at close**: Ideal for seller. Premium for "clean" deal
- **Earnout**: Portion of payment tied to post-acquisition performance. Risk for seller
- **Seller financing**: Seller acts as bank, buyer pays over time. Risk but may enable higher total price
- **Equity in acquirer**: Receive shares instead of cash. Only if you believe in acquirer's future

**Transition Period**: Usually 3-12 months where seller helps transition knowledge.
- FeedbackPanda: transition was painless due to SOPs and documentation
- Well-documented business → shorter transition → less risk for buyer → premium

### Negotiation Principles `[ZTS:EX.4.1]`

- **Multiple offers** → leverage. Never negotiate with only one buyer
- **Know your BATNA** (Best Alternative To Negotiated Agreement) → walking away is always an option
- **Valuation anchoring**: Let buyer make first offer if possible
- **Protect your customers**: Include terms that ensure continued service
- **Legal representation**: ALWAYS have a lawyer review the deal. Not optional

## Transition Planning `[ZTS:EX.5]`

### Pre-Transition Checklist

**Documentation** (should already exist if you followed Built-to-Sell approach):
- [ ] All SOPs written and current
- [ ] Knowledge base comprehensive and up-to-date
- [ ] Technical documentation: architecture, deployment, monitoring
- [ ] Customer service playbook: common issues, escalation paths
- [ ] Financial records: clean, organized, reconciled
- [ ] Vendor/supplier list with contact information
- [ ] Legal: IP ownership clear, no outstanding disputes

**People**:
- [ ] Key employees informed (timing is critical)
- [ ] Transition roles defined: who trains whom
- [ ] Customer communication plan prepared
- [ ] Partner/vendor notification plan

### During Transition `[ZTS:EX.5.1]`

- **Train replacements thoroughly** — use your SOPs, do ride-alongs
- **Introduce new team to customers gradually** — maintain trust
- **Monitor metrics during transition** — catch any drops early
- **Be available but not indispensable** — gradual handoff

FeedbackPanda Transition Example:
- SOPs were pre-written → CS rep trained in days
- Documentation was comprehensive → developer onboarding smooth
- Customers were informed with clear messaging → minimal churn during transition
- "When I trained the CS rep our acquirer hired, all preparations paid off"

## Beyond Bootstrapping: Funding Options `[ZTS:EX.6]`

### When Bootstrapping Isn't Enough

**Semi-bootstrapped**: Exhibits bootstrapped behaviors (minimize expenses, automate, sustainable) but uses outside funding to accelerate specific parts.

**Indie-funded**: Non-traditional sources (crowd-investing, angel investors who understand lifestyle businesses). No hockey-stick growth expectations.

### Funding Decision Framework `[ZTS:EX.6.1]`

**Stay bootstrapped if**:
- You value independence and control
- Market supports gradual organic growth
- Revenue covers expenses and allows for reinvestment
- You don't need to "blitzscale" to win

**Consider funding if**:
- Winner-take-all market dynamics
- High upfront costs (hardware, compliance, inventory)
- Need to hire team faster than revenue allows
- Competitive pressure requires rapid expansion

**Critical warning**: Funding changes the game. VC expects 10x+ returns, which may conflict with building a sustainable, sellable business. Know what you're trading before you take money.

## The Four Stages Summary — Exit Readiness at Each Stage `[ZTS:GR.2]`

### Preparation ($0 revenue)
- **Exit readiness**: 0%. Focus on validation
- **What to do**: Audience → Problem → Solution → Product pipeline
- **Sellability action**: Start documenting decisions and processes from day one

### Survival (first customers, unstable revenue)
- **Exit readiness**: Low. Business not yet proven
- **What to do**: Find repeatable revenue model, establish pricing
- **Sellability action**: Begin automation, start SOPs for recurring tasks

### Stability (steady revenue, repeatable processes)
- **Exit readiness**: Medium. Business runs, but may still depend on founder
- **What to do**: Optimize processes, hire, build customer relationships
- **Sellability action**: Remove yourself from daily operations, document everything

### Growth (mature business, potential for exit)
- **Exit readiness**: High if Built-to-Sell principles followed
- **What to do**: Decide: keep running or sell
- **Sellability action**: Share metrics strategically, respond to acquisition interest

**Key Insight**: "We sold FeedbackPanda in mid-2019, just under two years after founding. It was an exciting and scary thing to do." The short timeline was possible because they built with intent from day one.

## Recession-Proof Exit Considerations `[ZTS:EX.7]`

- During recessions, valuations drop but good businesses still sell
- Cash reserve = negotiating power (you don't HAVE to sell)
- Buyers with deep pockets emerge during downturns (buying at discount)
- If your business survives a recession, it proves resilience → premium
- Document how you navigated the recession → shows management quality

**Decision Algorithm**:
- IF recession + cash reserve adequate + business growing → HOLD, sell later at premium
- IF recession + cash reserve low + business declining → SELL NOW, accept lower price
- IF recession + cash reserve adequate + business flat → NEGOTIATE from strength, don't rush
