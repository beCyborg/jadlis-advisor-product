# Audience, Email & the Growth Machine — Million Dollar Weekend (Kagan)

Covers the GROW IT playbook (chapters 6-8): build a 100-true-fans audience on one free platform, funnel it into an owned email list (the "ATM"), and run experiment-based marketing that doubles down on what works. Heavy on Kagan's own numbers.

> **Scope note (Disclaimer 3):** Kagan is the operator of an info-product funnel (AppSumo, 750k+ YouTube, 100k+ email). The channels below doubled as his own lead-gen and are cited as his wins. Take the tactics; treat "build a newsletter/YouTube" as one option, not the only path.

## [MDW:AUD] Social Media Is for Growth

### [MDW:AUD.1] 100 True Fans (smallest viable audience)
A community of **100 high-value, attentive fans** who know, like, and trust you dwarfs 10,000 low-value followers. Kagan calls on Kevin Kelly's "1,000 True Fans" and Seth Godin's "smallest viable audience."
- **Proof — Bo Bikes Bama:** Kagan asked his audience to donate; raised **$30,000 in three days** in small donations, *without going viral*. When he read the list of **102 donors, he recognized nearly every name** — people he'd interacted with and helped for free. (Gift levels ran $10 "Amigo" → $50 → $100 → $500 → $1,000 "Inner Circle" → $10,000 "BFF" Mexico City dinner.)
- Built through **generosity** — adding value without expectation, sometimes just a compliment. "I've spent twenty years giving out free content… so when I finally asked, saying yes was a no-brainer." It takes time.
- "People get hooked on CHARACTERS… do business with REAL PEOPLE." (Kagan never deliberately "built a personal brand.") Counter-example that it's not about being on camera: Danny Wang Design (lawn care) — 2.3M TikTok followers, 150k+ views on yard #transformations, and **Danny is never in the videos.**

### [MDW:AUD.2] Find Your Unique Angle
"What's your unique angle in thirty seconds or less?" — why would anyone care to read/watch you? Ben Kenyon (76ers strength coach) answered in four sentences that map to a formula:
1. **Who you are** — "I've been a performance coach for the last fourteen years…"
2. **Why you should trust him** — "…working with the best athletes in the world."
3. **What he's passionate about** — "Helping people perform better is my groove."
4. **What unique thing he'll do for you** — "…shift them into the mentality to dominate their life."

Challenge: write your own — (1) Who are you? (2) Why should people listen? (3) What are you passionate about? (4) What will you do for people? Then rewrite your bio to match. (Kagan's bio: "Chief Sumo at @AppSumo. #30 at Facebook. Helping entrepreneurs at Okdork.com.")

### [MDW:AUD.3] Pick ONE Platform
Choose on three criteria: (1) Which site has the audience you want? (2) Which medium do YOU enjoy creating in? (3) Where will you get disproportionate results for the work? Start with **one** as an experiment. Kagan's own eliminations (each with a counter-example that it *can* work for others):
- **Instagram** — he doesn't take pics (but interior designer Kelsey Hutchins gets most business there).
- **Podcasting** — highly engaged but near-impossible to grow (Jordan Harbinger built a 7-figure show).
- **LinkedIn** — great B2B audience, noisy, hard to go viral (Justin Welsh sold a course for **$1.3M in two years**).
- **Blogging** — search volume down (Matt Taibbi: **1.3M visitors/month, $500,000+/year on Substack**).
- **Twitter** — flat users, hard to convert off-platform (Nick Huber / Sweaty Startup grew his storage biz from provocative tweets).
- **TikTok** — "1 YouTube sub ≈ 25 on TikTok"; Kagan got 150k+ TikTok followers repurposing YouTube but **no direct business impact.**
- **YouTube (his pick)** — "LARGEST STREAMING VIDEO SITE": **122 million daily active users, a billion hours/day**, monetizes and hosts free. He built a **750,000+** subscriber channel **shirtless in his living room with an iPhone 12.** SunnyV2 has 2M+ subs and we've never seen his face. "No excuses."

### [MDW:AUD.4] The Content Circle Framework
Start narrow, expand outward — every circle still includes your core. **Ali Abdaal** (4.4M YouTube subs, **$400,000/month**) is the model:
1. **Core Circle** — the narrowest niche (Ali: British medical-school entrance exams, BMAT sections). "A niche within a niche."
2. **Medium Circle** — overlaps the core but broader (Ali: studying & productivity for all students).
3. **Large Circle** — the largest still-related audience (Ali: his salary, latest Apple products).

Formula: **outcome you'll deliver + target market.** Housecleaning example — Core: "how to clean your evaporative cooler + in the Southwest USA"; Medium: "how to choose laundry detergents + for new homeowners"; Large: "the ten best vacuums + for a family."
- **Dustin's Fish Tanks** — started literally reviewing fish tanks → all things fish → **150,000 subscribers, a million-dollar** online store.
- **Kyle Lasota (kylegotcamera)** — biohacking; only **16,000 subs** and many videos get just **400 views**, yet nets **$1M/year** from affiliate sales because his Core Circle LOVES him.
- **Andy Schneider (Chicken Whisperer)** — backyard chickens → radio show, magazine, book, national workshops.

**Unique viewpoint** questions: What does everyone think is true that you think is wrong? What is nobody in your market talking about that they should? What blind-spot mistakes are people making? Audiences want something relevant, useful, and *surprising*.

### [MDW:AUD.5] Be the Guide, Not the Guru
People don't want a lecturing guru; they want to tag along with a guide. Ali titles videos **"How I…"** not "How to…". "Document what YOU do, not what you think everyone else should do." Some of Kagan's most popular videos feature him **failing**. Co-create with your audience (the Sumo Jerky dare came from daring his Monthly1K class). LegalEagle's Devin Stone has viewers post "objections" he sustains/overrules. Matt's Off-Road Recovery (1.4M subs) is just a guy doing his tow-truck day job.

**First content challenge:** post one piece **today** — any format — using your Unique Angle + one Platform + your Content Circle. Don't worry about scripts, gear, or views.

## [MDW:EMAIL] Email Is for Profit

### [MDW:EMAIL.1] Why Email Beats Everything
Which is most valuable — 100 email subscribers, 1,000 YouTube subs, or 10,000 Instagram followers? **Answer: the 100 emails.** Six reasons:
1. AppSumo does **$65M/year** total transactions; **~50% comes from email**, consistent for 10+ years.
2. Kagan has 120k Twitter / 750k YouTube / 150k TikTok — "I would give them all up for my **100,000 email subscribers**." Every send, **40,000 people open** and consume; other platforms are 100 to 1M and not in his control.
3. Email is used by **4 billion people; 89% check every day** — the largest at-scale comms channel.
4. Algorithms can kill you: **LittleThings lost 75% of its 20,000,000 monthly visitors** in Facebook's 2018 change; CEO says he lost **$100M**.
5. You **own** the list forever — your "insurance policy."
6. It doesn't cost significant money to grow or use (unlike Facebook/Google ads).

**Quality > size.** A real-estate mogul bought **1,000,000 subscribers**; Charlie Hoehn emailed five messages and **fewer than 100 people opened them.** A **healthy list has a ~20% open rate** — target that. "You're not really building an audience if you don't have their email." #1 regret of nearly every entrepreneur: "I wish I started my email list sooner."

### [MDW:EMAIL.2] Getting Your First 100 Subscribers
Set up a landing page first (SendFox/Mailchimp/ConvertKit/Webflow) — just a value proposition + an email box, optionally offering a Lead Magnet.

- **0 → 10: The Dream Ten.** Your existing network. Template (Bryan Harris used it → **10,000+ subs at Videofruit**):
```
Hey [name]!
I just wanted to let you know that I'm starting [description of your new
business].
I'm going to publish [one article per week / a weekly tip] on how to
[subject].
Is this something you are interested in?
Here's an easy way to sign up! [landing page] Or you can just write back
with "Yes, dude, I'd like to," and I'll do it for you!
Hope things are great! [Your name]
```
- **11 → 50: Lazy Marketing.** Put the landing-page link in **every point of contact**: email signature + your Twitter/LinkedIn/TikTok/Instagram/Facebook bios. "On average, a person sends around **40 emails per day**" = 40 lottery tickets daily. (Track with Bitly/Linktree.)
- **51 → 100: Post in Your Places.** Post a short version wherever you're active:
```
Hey Everyone
Starting a "weekly" newsletter about [subject].
Go to website.com to join the newsletter.
```
Plus **targeted referrals** — ask friends to refer one *specific* person: "Do you know someone who makes purchase decisions at an office — and likes a good laugh?"

### [MDW:EMAIL.3] The Lead Magnet
A free, useful piece of content that incentivizes signup vs. just asking. **Chris Von Wilpert** spent **40 hours** on a blog post reverse-engineering HubSpot's content marketing, drove **5,000 views**, and grew from **0 to 1,000+ subscribers in two weeks** with a "growth hacks" download as the magnet. (It later became a **$100,000** engagement with AppSumo.) Magnets don't need to be that big — checklist, template, advanced guide, or a free book (Kagan's: *11 Side Hustle Ideas to Make $500/Day from Your Phone*). Jess Dante's free **London 101 Guide** was downloaded **45,000+ times**. Nick True (Mapped Out Money) gets the highest signup with a **checklist that relates to the video**. Don't spend more than **two hours** on the first iteration — start SMALL.

### [MDW:EMAIL.4] The Autoresponder Sequence (send your best stuff first)
Open rates start high then decline, so front-load value. Three-email progression:

**1. Welcome Email** — a bear hug + set expectations + **ask one question**:
```
SUBJECT: You're awesome
Thanks for joining OkDork. You are awesome!
Over 17 years of working online I've learned some things:
- #30 at Facebook and helped launch mobile, status updates, and more
- #4 at Mint and led growth to 1 million users within 1 year
- Started AppSumo, which is now an $85M/year business
And now, I want to help you on your journey to living the life you want.
What could I write to provide value to you?
Love you, Noah "tacos" Kagan
```

**2. Connection Email** — ask them to connect on social:
```
SUBJECT: Connect with me on LinkedIn
Howdy amigo,
Send me a connection request on LinkedIn to help share our relationships,
see behind-the-scenes thoughts on marketing, startups, and more…
Hugs, Noah
```

**3. Content Email** — give your single best piece:
```
SUBJECT: Starting an 8-figure business with $50
I started AppSumo in March 2010.
In one weekend, with $50, I launched version 1 of the site. It was simple.
12 years later, Sumo Group has grown into an 8-figure business.
Starting a business can be hard. But I want to show you an easier way:
Here's how I built AppSumo.com for $50.
Enjoy, Noah
```
**One-by-one marketing:** respond to *every single* new subscriber, especially early. Kagan still does this for nearly every email.

### [MDW:EMAIL.5] Put Personality in the Copy
AppSumo's first $10,000 day came from letting copywriter **Neville Medhora** rewrite a dull "Go buy!" email into a funny, story-driven one about fonts (Steve Jobs + typography). Same offer, better copy → **$9,563 profit in 24 hours — nearly 100× the usual ~$100/email.** "Sales go UP when the people selling are enjoying themselves." It "gave me permission to make who I am a part of how I market and sell."

## [MDW:GROW] The Growth Machine (Experiment-Based Marketing)
Kagan's plan grew Mint from **0 to 1 million users in six months** (he promised Aaron Patzer 100,000 and delivered 1M — "If I don't hit my target, you don't have to pay me"). He's used the same plan across 8 businesses. Five questions:
1. What is your **one goal** for this year?
2. Who exactly is your customer and **where** can you find them?
3. What is **one** marketing activity you can double down on?
4. How can you **delight your first 100** customers?
5. If you HAD to **double your business with no money in 30 days**, what would you do?

> "Merely copying the plan and then hoping and praying won't work. That's gambling." Blogging worked at Mint but never at AppSumo; paid ads work at AppSumo but not OkDork. Set up a *process* to find what works for *you*.

Same plan, other results: **Sumo.com** reached **1 billion impressions in 12 months**; **SendFox.com** got **10,000 customers in 6 months** and **850,000+ YouTube subscribers**. The plan is repeatable; the winning *channel* is not — you find it by testing.

### [MDW:GROW.2] One Hyper-Focused Goal (number + timeframe)
Zuckerberg wrote **GROWTH → 1 BILLION** on the whiteboard and killed everything else. Pick one number that everything else follows from (AppSumo's early goal: **100,000 email addresses** — "if we could grow that number, everything else grew"). Add a timeframe ("worth $1M **in three years**"), then break into monthly targets and **WORK BACKWARDS FROM YOUR GOAL.**

### [MDW:GROW.3] The Marketing Experiment List — Daniel Bliss case
Guess **expected sales per channel**, run them, log **actual sales**. Daniel (belay glasses) worked backwards: **$4,000/mo goal ÷ $24 profit per $60 pair = 166 glasses/month** (~5-6/day). With only 1 hour/week he chose his top-2 expected channels (personal network + wholesale). Thirty-day result:

| Source | Expected | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Reach out to friends + referrals | 30 | 9 |
| Sale to Vancouver Rock Climbing Group | 20 | 11 |
| **Wholesale options** | 50 | **217** |
| Marketplaces — eBay | 25 | 0 |
| Giveaways | 25 | 0 |
| Facebook ads | 16 | 0 |
| **Total** | **166 (goal)** | **237** |

A single **Sierra Trading** wholesale order was **$4,200**. Lesson: "You never know what's going to work." He thought wholesale would do 50; it did 200+. He thought eBay would do 30; it did 0. So he doubled down on wholesale (90% of sales). Include a **Time** column (which activities are fast) and generally **don't spend money at first — exhaust free options.** Best guesses are fine; the point is to prioritize.

**Finding customers:** define your ideal customer by patterns in existing ones (AppSumo = "Marketing Agency Matt, a solopreneur"; OkDork = "an underdog looking for inspiration"). Ask existing customers where to find more: *"Where's the one specific place you'd expect to learn about my product?"*

### [MDW:GROW.4] Double Down on What Works; Kill What Doesn't (the lazy mindset)
"Find what works and double down on it; find what doesn't work and kill it." Even Kagan forgets: **Sumo.com pushed Instagram for six months and $20,000 → drove literally $0** before killing it. Give a channel ~**30 days**; be ruthless (even $100/day or 30 min/day is opportunity cost). When something works, do more of it until it stops. "Have a **lazy mindset**: if something's too hard and not working after a good try, give up and move on." In 2019 Kagan tried Twitter/TikTok/blogging/Instagram/YouTube for 30 days, then went **all in on YouTube.**

### [MDW:GROW.5] Delight Your First 100 Customers
"The biggest growth lever in business is customer **retention and referrals**." If you're starting out, every referral can double your business. Do unscalable things — that's the point:
- Kagan personally replied to **every YouTube comment**.
- With **Gambit** he gave his **personal phone number to every customer** even at **$20M+/year**.
- He still personally emails AppSumo customers to ask what they like/dislike (verbatim early example: "What made you interested in buying Imgur? What other services would you want big discounts on? Any suggestions for the site?").
- **Nick Bare** (Bare Performance Nutrition) messaged every customer at 4 a.m. from a Korea deployment → 7-figure supplement company.
- The magic question to each customer: **"What is one thing I can do today that will make you twice as happy with us?"**

## [MDW:SYS] Systems That Sustain the Machine (52 Chances)
Growth dies without a system to keep showing up. Kagan's chapter 9: "In a j-o-b you accept the system you're in. As an entrepreneur, you design your own." You get **52 chances a year** (weekly reps).

### [MDW:SYS.1] Dream Year → Goals
Write a detailed, specific **Dream Year** (where you live, what you do, how you feel, travel), then pull the items you're *most* excited about into goals across four buckets: **Work, Health, Personal, Travel.** "If you're hesitating on a dream, it shouldn't make your goals list." Aim for sustainable, not super-aggressive ("more impressive to stick with something than burn out after one year"). Keep goals visible — phone lock screen, sticky note, bathroom mirror, a weekly text file.

### [MDW:SYS.2] Color Your Calendar
"Show me your calendar and I'll tell you what's most important to you." We all get **168 hours/week**. Put every item in a category, color-code (Blue = Work, Green = Health, Purple = Personal, Yellow = Travel), and glance to check: "My stated priority is 500k YouTube subs — so why is there so little blue?" **Front-load priorities** early in the week (Kagan puts key tasks Mon/Tue). Put recurring important tasks **on repeat** so they become automatic and free your brain.

### [MDW:SYS.3] Accountability Buddy + Sunday (P)review
"We make better choices when someone is observing" (the Hawthorne effect — Kagan's #1 productivity hack). Every Sunday for 10+ years he emails **Adam Gilbert** a (P)review: what he said he'd do last week, how much he did, and next week's plan. A good buddy **replies and calls you out** — Adam once asked, "How much time are you spending on your push-ups? I don't see them listed and you said it was an important goal." Spend ~15 minutes each Sunday reviewing last week's goals against results — "not to judge or shame, but to stay accountable and improve."

## Growth path recap
Audience (free, one platform, 100 true fans) → capture email (owned ATM, Lead Magnet + autoresponder) → run 30-day marketing experiments and double down → sustain with a weekly system and an accountability buddy. Underpinning all of it is `[MDW:L100]` the **Law of 100** (see `references/validation-48h.md`) — do 100 reps of your chosen channel before judging it. Kagan quit his own podcast at 50 episodes; Buffer stuck it out to **$20M** recurring revenue.
