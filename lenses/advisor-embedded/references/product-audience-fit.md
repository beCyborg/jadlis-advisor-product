# The Embedded Entrepreneur: Product-Audience Fit — Finding Problems, Validation, Pricing

Source: "The Embedded Entrepreneur" by Arvid Kahl (2021), Part IV (Problem Discovery) + Validation chapters

---

## Problem Discovery `[EE:PD]`

### Goal

Найти критические проблемы внутри сообществ, в которые ты embedded. Результат: deep understanding одной или нескольких critical problems, expressed by community members.

**Problem Discovery is over** when you have selected a problem to solve and started working on a solution.

### Properties of an Interesting Problem `[EE:PD.1]`

**Definition**: "Problem" = anything that stands in the way of people accomplishing their goals. Pain you can relieve, job that needs doing, gap you can fill.

**Three Kinds of Problems**:

1. **Time-related** `[EE:PD.2]`: "This takes too long. This happens too often."
   - Productivity issues, tedious chores, inefficiency
   - People complain about tedium → time-related problem

2. **Resource-related** `[EE:PD.3]`: "We can't afford this. Too many people are working on this."
   - Waste of money, prohibitive costs, compliance, wrong people on wrong tasks
   - People complain about costs → resource-related problem

3. **Problems of the Self** `[EE:PD.4]`: "This makes us look bad. This prevents progress."
   - Reputation, Accomplishment, Advancement, Empowerment
   - Most time/resource problems trace BACK to self-problems:
     * Expensive software purchase → buyer looks incompetent (reputation)
     * Slow inventory report → person looks lazy (advancement)

### Properties of Critical Problems `[EE:PD.5]`

The more boxes checked → more likely customers will pay:

- [ ] **Painful** — involves loss, quality of life impacted, HURTS
- [ ] **Real and measurable pain** — every occurrence causes frustration. Non-optional, can't opt out or delegate
- [ ] **Frequent and recurring** — keeps coming back, every time same effort
- [ ] **Non-negligible time** — can't solve quickly, deferring makes it worse. Unwelcome chore: important yet tiresome

**Value test**: People gladly pay when paying < continuing with current approach. They pay if solution:
- Saves time
- Saves money
- Makes them money
(All three = jackpot)

## The Shape of a Problem in the Wild `[EE:PA]`

### Prospect Awareness Scale `[EE:PA]`

(Eugene Schwartz — adapted for community observation)

| Level | Description | Signal in Community | What They Say |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Completely Unaware** `[EE:PA.1]` | Don't know they have problem | **Complaints** | "I can't believe X is so hard..." |
| **Problem-Aware** `[EE:PA.2]` | Know problem, not solutions | **Asking for help** | "How do people deal with X?" |
| **Solution-Aware** `[EE:PA.3]` | Know solutions exist, not which | **Asking for recommendations** | "What tools do you use for X?" |
| **Product-Aware** `[EE:PA.4]` | Know specific products, comparing | **Asking for alternatives** | "What's a good alternative to X?" |
| **Most Aware** `[EE:PA.5]` | Know your product, need deal | Not useful for problem discovery | — |

### Reading Community Signals `[EE:PA.6]`

**Complaints** `[EE:PA.1]`:
- "I can't believe X is so hard..."
- "Why is there no X for Y..."
- "I can't figure out how to X..."
- Explosive message, may overstate. BUT: regular complaints about same issue = very critical problem
- Tip: Don't engage immediately. Note down, engage later through different channel

**Asking for Help** `[EE:PA.2]`:
- Exhausted their repertoire. In professional communities — may be sign of weakness (threshold varies)
- Pay attention when EXPERTS ask for help → critical problem even experienced can't solve
- Follow the HELPERS — they know the problem space deeply

**Looking for Recommendations** `[EE:PA.3]`:
- Note: What's recommended most often? What's recommended repeatedly across many questions?
- Who recommends and why? Ulterior motives?
- How are recommended solutions monetized? (All free → trouble charging later)

**Looking for Alternatives** `[EE:PA.4]`:
- MOST interesting for problem discovery: validated problem + failed solution
- Pre-validates: problem exists, budget exists, someone tried to solve it
- Ask about workflow → understand WHY current solution doesn't fit

**DIY Systems** — People sharing crude Excel/Google Sheets solutions → recurring, painful enough to build system around

**Hiring** — Looking to hire someone for a job → couldn't deal with problem, chose to act

## Budgets and Purchasing Agency `[EE:PAF.2]`

### Who Can Pay?

**Key distinction**: Person who USES product ≠ person who PAYS for product (sometimes)

**Salaried employees**: No budget insight, no purchasing agency. Need boss approval. Marketing must convince DECISION-MAKERS.

**Freelancers**: Own boss, own budget, own decision. But budget competes with personal expenses (mortgage, food). Need to justify EVERY tool purchase.

**Gig economy trap** (FeedbackPanda): Teachers didn't understand they were self-employed entrepreneurs. Had to create awareness that they could/should budget for professional tools.

**Decision Algorithm**:
- LOOK FOR: People asking for recommendations/alternatives (higher on Awareness Scale = less education needed)
- PREFER: Markets where budgets for solutions already exist
- INVESTIGATE: Who makes purchasing decision? How flexible is budget?
- WARNING: If consensus in community is "this should be free" → beware. Convincing = uphill battle

## Validation/Invalidation Principle `[EE:PAF.1]`

### The Core Insight

**You cannot prove theories.** Million reasons you're right, but single valid counterexample disproves everything. (Karl Popper — falsification)

**In business terms**: You can spend months finding people who maybe want your product. OR you can try to find out why people DON'T need it.

**Validation through invalidation** = try to invalidate riskiest assumptions first (Eric Ries). What survives thorough invalidation → work with.

### Validation vs Guaranteed Success

**There is no guaranteed success.** Validation doesn't mean you'll succeed. It means you've become less uncertain. Important distinction — affects how you weigh risks.

**Danger of misunderstood validation**: "Validated" assumptions stop being questioned → products built on shaky foundations → business crumbles because we only looked at happy path.

**Marble block metaphor**: Carve away until statue emerges. It might be a different statue than planned — but it will be a statue.

### The Mom Test Connection

(Rob Fitzpatrick): Parents will encourage because that's their job. Strangers will show support too. Words are cheap.

**Solution**: Don't ask if they LIKE your idea. Have them talk about their PROBLEMS.
- NOT: "Do you think this is a good idea?"
- YES: "What problems do you encounter when trying to X?"
- YES: "Which tools do you use more than Y times a week?"
- YES: "How often do you run into problem X while doing Y?"

Give prospects opportunity to share WHOLE STORY, not just figures you want.

## Finding the Problem You Want to Solve `[EE:PD.6]`

### Decision Framework

After Embedded Exploration, you have many noted problems. Now decide which to focus on.

**Criteria**:
1. Criticality score (from checklist above)
2. Frequency in community conversations
3. Your ability to solve it (skills, resources)
4. Your passion for the solution
5. Budget exists in audience
6. Size of affected audience segment

### Building Solution is NOT Solitary

Like standup comedian — test ideas in front of audience. Iterate, change, improve. Feedback + interaction = fundamental.

**Self-supporting construct**: Gather attention → work on solution → invites more people → more insights → better product → more attention.

## Actionable Frameworks for Problem Validation `[EE:PAF.3]`

### Community-Based Validation Checklist

- [ ] Problem mentioned by 3+ independent people in community
- [ ] People creating DIY solutions (Excel, Google Sheets, Post-its)
- [ ] People actively looking for recommendations/alternatives
- [ ] Experts (not just beginners) struggling with this problem
- [ ] Someone has hired/tried to hire a person to solve this
- [ ] People willing to jump on a call to discuss it
- [ ] Budget indicators exist (paid products in space, asking about pricing)

### Questions for Deep Problem Exploration

**In community (public)**:
- "What problems do you run into over and over again? Has anyone tried to solve them?"
- "Do you spend money on solutions to problems discussed here?"
- "Who should I reach out to learn more about this industry?"

**In 1-on-1 conversations (DM/call)**:
- "Walk me through your typical workday. Where do you get stuck?"
- "What's the most time-consuming part of your work that feels wasteful?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix ONE thing, what would it be?"
- "How are you solving this problem right now? How much does that cost you?"
- "Have you ever tried a product that was supposed to help but didn't?"

### Solution Pre-Validation

Before building anything, check:
- [ ] Solution fits into existing workflow (doesn't add friction)
- [ ] Solution doesn't create new problems
- [ ] Cost of your solution < cost of current approach (time + money + self)
- [ ] People can switch to your solution without massive migration pain
- [ ] Solution works at the stage of workflow where they need it (mobile? desktop? offline?)

## Pricing Discovery `[EE:PAF.4]`

### How to Discover Price Willingness

1. **Look at existing solutions** — what do people already pay? → anchor price
2. **Ask for alternatives** conversations reveal budget expectations
3. **"How much does this cost you now?"** — calculate time + money of current approach
4. **Buffer approach**: Landing page with pricing tiers → measure which gets clicks before building product

### Pricing Principles from Community Observation

- If community recommends mostly free tools → need strong justification for paid
- If people ask for alternatives to expensive tools → price slightly below market + better fit
- If people describe problem but no tools exist → price based on value created (time saved × hourly rate)
- FeedbackPanda: Teachers saved 2 hours/day of unpaid work → $9/month was no-brainer → raised 50% after year, growth continued

## Common Problem Discovery Anti-Patterns `[EE:PD.7]`

1. **Asking leading questions** → tainted responses. Ask about problems, not your solution
2. **Only listening to beginners** → skewed toward novice problems. Experts have interesting budgets
3. **Quantitative surveys too early** → miss underlying reasons, can't steer conversation
4. **Echo chamber** → same few voices confirming each other. Seek diverse perspectives
5. **Ignoring negative data** → "this doesn't validate my idea so I'll ignore it" = path to failure
6. **Moving too fast from observation to solution** → spend more time understanding before building
7. **Confusing feature requests with problems** → people request features based on tools they know. Dig deeper to find underlying need
